## 1. The question

When a class name is composed of orthogonal facets
(`Pipeline + <Subject> + <Operation | ToTarget> + [Qualifier]`), one decision governs the whole
grammar: **which facet leads?**

The framing question of the research was: *is there an objective, measurable criterion for facet
order in a class name, or is it necessarily a matter of convention?* That is why both information
theory and classification theory were consulted, not just style guides.

## 2. Method and its limits

Sources were gathered by a **targeted review of canonical works**. There was no exhaustive
database search, no formal inclusion/exclusion protocol, and no claim of coverage. The level of
rigour is therefore **traceability of every claim to a founding source**, not **completeness of
the field** — stated explicitly so the review can be revised and, if needed, raised to a
systematic protocol later.

| Layer | Guiding questions | Selected source |
|---|---|---|
| Information theory | How is a token's discriminating power measured? Why does order affect reading efficiency? | Shannon (1948) — entropy, prefix codes, mutual information |
| Library classification | Is there a discipline that already prescribes facet order? What does it optimise? | Ranganathan (1937) — PMEST, collocation |
| Cognitive psychology | Is there a natural level at which people categorise? | Rosch (1978) — basic-level categories; Least Astonishment |
| Structural alignment | Should naming follow code structure? | Conway (1968); Stable Dependencies Principle |

## 3. Four pillars

### 3.1 Information theory — order by descending entropy

A hierarchical name behaves like a **prefix code**. The reader resolves it token by token, and the
prefix is most efficient when the facet that **branches most** (highest marginal entropy) comes
first, because on average it narrows the candidate space fastest.

This is **measurable, not aesthetic**. Measured over the current catalogue of pipeline classes,
the **Subject** facet carries **higher marginal entropy and higher efficiency** than the
**Operation** facet, so leading with Subject resolves the most uncertainty earliest.

> **The figures are not quoted here** (D-13). They are regenerated by the lint from the live
> class list — see §5.

**A second measurement qualifies the result.** The mutual information of the two facets is high:
the facets are **not fully orthogonal** in practice. One cluster (named-entity recognition) makes
up roughly half the catalogue and is uniformly `Extract`, so within it the discriminating
information has migrated into the **Qualifier** (engine, language). The two-orthogonal-axes
assumption holds everywhere except in that cluster, which is marked a **structural exception, not
a counterexample**.

### 3.2 Library classification — PMEST and collocation

Faceted classification prescribes a fixed citation order: **Personality – Matter – Energy – Space
– Time**. *Personality* (the thing itself — Subject) precedes *Energy* (the process acting on it
— Operation).

The governing reason is **collocation**: someone looking for everything about a given subject
expects it gathered in one place, not scattered across operations.

*Limit:* the argument is conditional on retrieval being dominantly **by subject** rather than by
operation. Transferring the library shelf/notation order to class naming is **analogical, not
literal**.

### 3.3 Cognitive psychology — basic-level categories

People spontaneously categorise at the "basic level" that is most informative for the task. The
relevant question is therefore **empirical, about the team's mental model**: when an engineer
needs a class, is the first thought *"I need something for PDF"* (Subject-led) or *"I need
something that extracts"* (Operation-led)?

**This cannot be settled by logic; it must be observed.** Least Astonishment then demands the
grammar follow what the team already thinks, regardless of theoretical preference.

### 3.4 Structural alignment — Conway and Stable Dependencies

If modules are already organised by Subject (`pdf/`, `png/`, `cad/`), a name beginning with the
Subject **mirrors the physical structure**, so name and path coincide
(`pipelines/pdf/…PipelinePDF…`). That closes the cognitive gap between navigating files and
reading names — a **homomorphism of the name space and the file space**, so one taxonomy serves
both instead of two rival ones.

## 4. Resolving the conflict

Information theory (§3.1) and the cognitive model (§3.3) **can diverge**: the mathematically most
informative order is not necessarily the one the team reads intuitively.

> Where they conflict, **Least Astonishment (§3.3) and structural alignment (§3.4) take
> precedence over raw entropy** — because the purpose of a name is **predictability** for the
> human and machine minds navigating by it, not maximal compression.

The methodology is therefore: compute facet entropy from the existing catalogue as an
**objective input** (§3.1), corroborated by collocation (§3.2) and structural alignment (§3.4);
decide the leading axis from the **observed mental model of the team** (§3.3) where it is in
tension with the measurement.

For the current catalogue the measurement indicates **Subject-first**, and §3.2 and §3.4 agree;
§3.3 is the deciding vote when they do not.

**Consequence for DRY.** Repeating the subject in both the path and the name is a **deliberate,
accepted trade-off** of DRY for self-description and collocation — see §3.4.

## 5. Limits and reproducibility

* The entropy and mutual-information figures depend on the **frequency distribution of the
  catalogue**, which must be **regenerated by the lint** from the live class list, not quoted
  statically. The raw distribution should be attached as a data file so the figures are
  **independently checkable**.
* The catalogue is **small** and dominated by a single cluster (`nlp`, a currently deferred
  module). Empirical entropy is **biased on small samples**; the figures must be recomputed as
  the catalogue grows.
* The §3.3 "deciding vote" (the team's mental model) **has not been measured**. The current
  decision rests on the agreement of §3.1, §3.2 and §3.4, with §3.3 as a **future empirical
  check** (a card sort, or autocomplete/search telemetry).
